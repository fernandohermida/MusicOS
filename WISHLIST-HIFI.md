# Hi-Fi / Audiophile Album Wishlist

## 1. The records that keep coming back

* [*] **01. Steely Dan — Aja (1977)**
  *Extremely precise studio production; great for imaging, separation, drums, bass and tiny details in dense arrangements.*

* [*] **02. Pink Floyd — The Dark Side of the Moon (1973)**
  *Huge soundstage, deep bass, stereo effects, ambience and dynamic changes make it an excellent all-round system test.*

* [*] **03. Daft Punk — Random Access Memories (2013)**
  *Exceptionally clean modern production combining live drums, bass, guitars, strings and electronics; excellent for layers and dynamics.*

* [ ] **04. Dire Straits — Brothers in Arms (1985)**
  *Famous for its spacious digital-era production, clean guitar textures, deep bass and very clear instrumental separation.*

* [ ] **05. Pink Floyd — Wish You Were Here (1975)**
  *Outstanding sense of space and atmosphere, with long instrumental passages that reveal soundstage depth and ambience.*

* [*] **06. Beck — Sea Change (2002)**
  *Lush and spacious production with intimate vocals, deep bass and strings; excellent for detail, texture and emotional atmosphere.*

* [*] **07. Miles Davis — Kind of Blue (1959)**
  *A reference for natural acoustic sound: trumpet, piano, bass and drums have realistic tone, placement and room ambience.*

* [ ] **08. Donald Fagen — The Nightfly (1982)**
  *One of the classic early digital recordings; exceptionally clean, precise and layered, with excellent vocals, percussion and keyboards.*

* [ ] **09. Cowboy Junkies — The Trinity Session (1988)**
  *Recorded around a single microphone in a church, producing an unusually coherent, natural and believable sense of musicians in a real space.*

* [ ] **10. Roger Waters — Amused to Death (1992)**
  *A famous headphone and speaker demo because of its extreme spatial effects, deep bass and cinematic stereo presentation.*

## 2. The classic audiophile collection

* [ ] **11. Fleetwood Mac — Rumours (1977)**
  *Dense vocal harmonies, guitars, bass and percussion remain remarkably clear; great for midrange transparency and separation.*

* [ ] **12. The Alan Parsons Project — I Robot (1977)**
  *Highly polished studio production with synths, bass, percussion and layered effects; excellent for precision and stereo placement.*

* [ ] **13. Supertramp — Crime of the Century (1974)**
  *Strong piano, vocals, drums and keyboards with impressive dynamics and separation, especially during the quieter-to-louder transitions.*

* [ ] **14. Jennifer Warnes — Famous Blue Raincoat (1987)**
  *A classic audiophile vocal recording with exceptionally clean female vocals, acoustic instruments and spacious production.*

* [ ] **15. Arne Domnérus — Jazz at the Pawnshop (1977)**
  *Famous for realistic live ambience; you can hear the room, audience and musicians together in a remarkably convincing space.*

* [*] **16. Dave Brubeck Quartet — Time Out (1959)**
  *Excellent natural jazz recording with realistic piano, saxophone, double bass and drums; ideal for timbre and imaging.*

* [ ] **17. Roxy Music — Avalon (1982)**
  *Atmospheric 1980s production with beautiful reverb, synths, guitar and percussion; excellent for spaciousness and low-level detail.*

* [*] **18. Massive Attack — Mezzanine (1998)**
  *Exceptional electronic bass and atmosphere; great for testing sub-bass extension, control, layering and texture.*

* [ ] **19. The Beatles — Abbey Road (1969)**
  *Brilliant studio craftsmanship with layered vocals, guitars, drums and the famous medley; revealing without being overly clinical.*

* [ ] **20. Steely Dan — Gaucho (1980)**
  *Extremely dense but controlled production; excellent for seeing whether a system can preserve separation when many instruments play together.*

## 3. Vocals, acoustic instruments and live realism

* [ ] **21. Jeff Buckley — Grace (1994)**
  *Huge vocal dynamics, detailed acoustic guitar and powerful contrasts between intimate and explosive passages.*

* [ ] **22. Norah Jones — Come Away With Me (2002)**
  *Intimate vocals and acoustic instruments with a soft natural presentation; very useful for judging vocal realism and midrange quality.*

* [ ] **23. Eric Clapton — Unplugged (1992)**
  *Excellent acoustic guitar transients, vocal presence and live-room ambience; great for judging natural instrument texture.*

* [ ] **24. Nils Lofgren — Acoustic Live (1997)**
  *A guitar-reference recording; finger attack, string texture, dynamics and live spatial information are extremely revealing.*

* [ ] **25. Hugh Masekela — Hope (1994)**
  *Explosive live dynamics, percussion and brass combined with a convincing sense of the performance space.*

* [ ] **26. Tool — Lateralus (2001)**
  *Massive drums, bass and layered guitars remain surprisingly intelligible; excellent for dynamics and separation in complex rock.*

* [ ] **27. Michael Jackson — Thriller (1982)**
  *Polished Quincy Jones production with punchy drums, bass, vocals and layers of studio detail; an excellent all-round pop reference.*

* [ ] **28. Lorde — Pure Heroine (2013)**
  *Sparse modern production makes sub-bass, vocal texture, silence and low-level details particularly easy to hear.*

* [ ] **29. Yosi Horikawa — Vapor (2013)**
  *Excellent headphone-oriented electronic recording with unusual spatial effects, transient sounds and precisely positioned elements.*

* [*] **30. Radiohead — Kid A (2000)**
  *Combines electronic and acoustic textures in a spacious, complex mix that is very revealing of layering and imaging.*

## 4. Alternative, electronic and beautifully engineered albums

* [ ] **31. Talk Talk — The Colour of Spring (1986)**
  *Warm analogue textures, beautiful percussion and lots of air around instruments; excellent for depth and musical realism.*

* [ ] **32. Talk Talk — Spirit of Eden (1988)**
  *Extremely dynamic and atmospheric, moving from near silence to large acoustic events; excellent for microdynamics and soundstage.*

* [ ] **33. Peter Gabriel — So (1986)**
  *Huge drums, percussion, vocals and synths with carefully controlled ambience; a strong example of great 1980s production.*

* [ ] **34. The Blue Nile — Hats (1989)**
  *Minimal, atmospheric production where synth ambience, vocals and subtle percussion expose low-level detail and space.*

* [ ] **35. Sade — Diamond Life (1984)**
  *Smooth and uncluttered production with excellent bass, percussion and vocal placement; very easy to hear changes in tonal balance.*

* [ ] **36. R.E.M. — Automatic for the People (1992)**
  *Spacious guitars, strings, percussion and vocals with a restrained production style that rewards a transparent system.*

* [ ] **37. Pat Metheny Group — Still Life (Talking) (1987)**
  *Rich guitar, percussion, vocals and Brazilian-influenced rhythms create a wide and layered stereo field.*

* [ ] **38. Depeche Mode — Violator (1990)**
  *Deep electronic bass, dark synth textures and reverberant spaces make it particularly good for headphones and bass evaluation.*

* [ ] **39. Leftfield — Leftism (1995)**
  *Great electronic reference for rhythm, low-end pressure, stereo effects and dense but controlled production.*

* [ ] **40. Nine Inch Nails — The Fragile (1999)**
  *Extremely layered industrial production; excellent for testing separation and whether complex mixes become congested.*

## 5. Jazz, blues and classical reference recordings

* [ ] **41. Muddy Waters — Folk Singer (1964)**
  *Minimalist blues recording with highly realistic voice, guitar, bass and percussion; excellent for natural presence and timbre.*

* [ ] **42. Ella Fitzgerald & Louis Armstrong — Ella and Louis (1956)**
  *Beautifully realistic vocal recording with distinct vocal textures and believable room positioning.*

* [*] **43. Stan Getz & João Gilberto — Getz/Gilberto (1964)**
  *Intimate bossa nova recording with saxophone, vocals, guitar and percussion; excellent for natural tone and subtle detail.*

* [ ] **44. Jimmy Smith — Back at the Chicken Shack (1960)**
  *Classic Rudy Van Gelder jazz recording with Hammond organ, guitar, saxophone and drums; great for instrumental definition and energy.*

* [ ] **45. Charles Mingus — Mingus Ah Um (1959)**
  *Larger ensemble with busy brass and rhythm arrangements; useful for testing separation when many instruments are active.*

* [ ] **46. Holst — The Planets — Zubin Mehta / Los Angeles Philharmonic**
  *Huge orchestral scale, powerful percussion and wide dynamics; excellent for testing soundstage size and low-frequency authority.*

* [ ] **47. Rachmaninoff — Symphonic Dances / Vocalise — Eiji Oue / Minnesota Orchestra**
  *A major modern classical reference with huge dynamics, deep bass and an exceptionally clean orchestral soundstage.*

* [ ] **48. Stravinsky — The Rite of Spring / The Firebird / The Song of the Nightingale — Eiji Oue / Minnesota Orchestra**
  *Explosive orchestral dynamics, percussion and brass make this excellent for testing scale, transient response and complex layering.*

* [ ] **49. Miles Davis — Bitches Brew (1970)**
  *Dense psychedelic electric-jazz production with unusual stereo positioning, bass and percussion; excellent for complex-layer separation.*

* [ ] **50. Darkside — Psychic (2013)**
  *Deep bass, electronic textures and organic instruments combine into a large, immersive and atmospheric modern production.*
